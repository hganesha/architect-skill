# Architectural Grammar — how spaces relate

Adjustable conventions, not universal laws. Style packs may override them.

## Zoning
- **Public** (living, dining, foyer, kitchen): convenient access from entrance;
  sequence entrance → foyer → living should not cross private zones.
- **Private** (bedrooms, study): quiet side, opposite or buffered from entrance;
  cluster bedrooms; bedrooms need an exterior/courtyard wall (daylight availability proxy).
- **Service/wet** (bathrooms, utility, kitchen): share plumbing walls where
  possible; stack wet rooms vertically across levels; ventilate to exterior.
- **Circulation** (hall, gallery, stair): connect, never terminate in a dead end
  > 3 m where possible; galleries around courtyards are circulation *and* daylight devices.
- **Outdoor** (courtyard, terrace): open-to-sky spaces are voids in roof/upper
  floors; surrounding rooms borrow light from them.

## Adjacency defaults
living↔dining↔kitchen (open-plan cluster) · kitchen↔utility · bedroom↔bath ·
foyer↔living · stair↔circulation on *both* levels it connects.

## Light & air (concept-level proxies only)
Habitable room = at least one exterior or courtyard wall with glazing.
Deep plans (> 8 m from a lit edge) need a courtyard, skylight, or double height.
South/north strategy flips with hemisphere — record site hemisphere in the spec.

## Structure (concept-level)
Keep a plausible column/wall grid (3–6 m bays); avoid floating long spans in the
narrative; stairs need straight-run length = treads × going + landings.
State "concept structure" — never sizes, never adequacy claims.

## Style packs (preferences layered on the grammar)
- `japanese-minimal`: low horizontal massing, deep eaves, timber screens, garden-first.
- `south-indian-courtyard`: central open court, veranda/gallery ring, stone + timber.
- `contemporary-tropical`: cross-ventilation, shaded openings, indoor-outdoor rooms.
Style changes materials, roof form and opening rhythm — never geometric validity.
